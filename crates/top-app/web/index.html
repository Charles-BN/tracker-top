Tracker technology

re-optimises every tracker

Pile types

Pile limits

Lowest and highest reveal (top of pile above the ground) of each pile part, e.g. a clearance under the modules, or the pile length minus its minimum embedment. Empty: no limit of its own. They narrow the window above.

How these are used

With heights on the torque tube, the optimiser works on the tube: the window, the angle limit, the set-out and the alignment of neighbouring rows all apply to it, and each pile's top is the tube minus its type's drop, rounded to the centimetre (what the installers drive to). The motor pile (the drive, ATI's centre structure) is the pile the set-out keeps fixed. A master list giving both tube and pile-top elevations (ATI: Z TT, Z Pile) fills this in on import, and its row heights (Row TTH) are used row by row.

Neighbouring rows

less shade

Tops are pulled towards the line through the rows on either side, inside the reveal window and every other limit. Blocks ticked as issued to the installers (Overview, Progress by block) keep their tops.

Why and how

When the trackers backtrack in the early morning and late evening, a row that sits lower than the line through its two neighbours, or whose height step to a neighbour changes along the row, gets shade the backtracking cannot avoid. A steady slope across the rows is fine. With this on, each tracker is optimised with a pull towards the line through its neighbours at the same Northing (beside one row only: parallel to it), aimed at least 2 cm inside the reveal window, so it never adds cut/fill; a tracker whose result would get worse (cut/fill, a limit) gets a weaker pull or none. Rows are solved again in rounds while their neighbours move. The Off rows column in Results gives each tracker's largest distance from that line.

Set-out

new pile positions
How set-out works

The layout's distance between two piles is the torque-tube span length. Where the tops differ in height, the span covers less ground, so piles move along the tracker towards the motor pile, which stays at its design position. The tracker is then re-optimised and re-checked at the new positions.
